letsfederate.org — content architecture blueprint

How the advocacy site is organized: three story arcs × three audiences × guided journeys, with every piece of content traceable to a demonstrable fedmgr capability (or an honestly labelled roadmap item). This is the map; the AI-infrastructure paper is the plumbing and the deep re-assessment is the raw material. Dated 2026-07-05; refreshed 2026-07-12 with the "why behind the choices" arc and the knowledge-assistant plan (§6–§7).

1 · The spine: three arcs

Every page, Q&A entry, and demo belongs to exactly one arc, so a visitor always knows which question they're answering. The arcs came straight out of the audit's websiteAngle harvest — the fixes to fedmgr's real gaps are the demonstrations.

ArcThe promiseThe proof (from the audit)
Deploy OIDF with AI Stand up verifiable trust for MCP and watch a chain resolve, admit, and revoke. The five-minute enroll→issue→admit→revoke loop; the hop-by-hop chain walk showing which key verifies which statement.
Bring your own trust You choose the anchors; no vendor is the chokepoint. A badge anyone can mint is not trust. The forged-jku trust-mark being rejected because its issuer has no chain to the anchor; the multi-anchor accepted-list.
Supply-chain integrity A trust mark proves this exact artifact passed your gate, verifiable offline. Digest-bound in-toto binding of SBOM + scan + mark; fedmgr signing its own release; the honest CBOM.

3 · Guided journeys

Journey A — "Curious → convinced" (local dev)

Home hero ("verify, don't assume") → Ask the docs: "can I try this locally?" → deploy-in-five-minutes answer → the enroll→issue→admit→revoke loop (CI-run demo) [roadmap: examples/02-trusted-mcp] → GitHub / fedmgr init

Journey B — "Assess → plan" (enterprise)

Scorecard (10 questions) → tier + per-section bars + guidance → weakest section deep-dive (e.g. supply-chain binding, revocation) → "who runs what" + "authenticate at scale" → (opt-in) guided AI assessment tier [roadmap: Dify+Langfuse] → contact / commercial letsfederate.com

Journey C — "Verify the claims" (operator)

Deep re-assessment (honest gaps, ranked) → oidf-spec conformance answers (trust_mark_type, resolve endpoint) → the trust-chain walkthrough → paste-a-trust-mark widget [roadmap: needs trust_mark_type on read] → GitHub issues / spec discussion

4 · The scorecard as the capture instrument

The scorecard is how we learn whether the thesis lands. It maps 1:1 onto the arcs, so a low score in a section routes the visitor to that arc's content, and the anonymous aggregate tells us which sections the market is weakest in (and therefore where our advocacy should concentrate).

Scorecard sectionFeeds arcWhat a low score triggers
Identity & accessDeploy OIDF with AIRoute to auth-at-scale; "start with a managed OIDC IdP".
Trust decisionsBring your own trustRoute to chain walkthrough + anchor model.
Supply-chain integritySupply-chain integrityRoute to SBOM→sign→gate→bind; "start with syft in CI".
AI & MCP exposureDeploy OIDF with AIRoute to gateway model; CBOM for the PQC question.

Capture posture (your decision): anonymous aggregate — client-side scoring, and on explicit consent a POST of answers + tier + pct only (no identity, no free text, credentials omitted). The endpoint is a Cloudflare Worker → D1; locally it's a no-op preview so the scorecard works offline. A weekly triage reads the aggregate + the Tier-1 "misses" (questions that retrieved poorly) + Langfuse traces from the opt-in tier, and every improvement lands as a content commit that re-mints the corpus.

6 · The "why behind the choices" arc — decisions as content

The build since the 2026-07 restructure produced a set of decisions that are themselves the most persuasive material we have: each one is a real fork where the easy path was wrong and we can show why. A trust product earns credibility by explaining its reasoning, not asserting conclusions — so every row below becomes a short "why we chose this" page, linked from the arc it serves and from the scorecard section it answers.

The choiceThe easy path we rejectedThe why (the page's thesis)Serves arc
packages/ vs services/ splitOne flat workspace; publish everything to npm Location must answer "npm library or deployable container?" — the boundary encodes the publish decision and stops a server being npm-published by accident. A structure test enforces it. Supply-chain integrity
Enforced SSC gate, not advisoryScan and warn; let humans decide A trust mark that can be issued over known HIGH CVEs is theater. The gate blocked at 11 HIGH, we remediated to 0/0/0/0, and proved the fix by exercising the risk path — not by re-scanning. Supply-chain integrity
FEDMGR_ALLOW_ORIGINS, not NODE_ENV=developmentA global dev flag to permit http endpoints A global switch permits http to every host, overloads a variable that changes unrelated behavior, is invisible to review, and gets copy-pasted into prod. Exact-origin exceptions are scoped, auditable, and empty-by-default. Residual risk is staleness, never forgery — the chain is signature-rooted. Bring your own trust
TLS via our own internal CAA public cert, or plaintext-in-the-lab forever The trust tool issues its own transport trust — the lab's TLS chain roots in a CA fedmgr minted and independently re-verified. Identity (a stable https URL) and location (where statements are fetched) are separable; TLS is hygiene, not the trust root. Deploy OIDF with AI
Disposable laptop labs, durable shared identitiesMake every lab identity permanent Re-minting a local federation is one command — so laptop identities are disposable. Only shared identities (org anchors, published MCPs, the trustmarket) must be minted once and never churn. This dissolves the "https everywhere vs local dev" tension. Deploy OIDF with AI
KeyProvider seam → OpenBao → cloud KMSHard-code SoftHSM; or jump straight to a vault Crawl/walk/run on one abstraction: encrypted-on-disk today, OpenBao ("safer than local, not an HSM") next, cloud KMS/HSM later — each a KeyProvider implementation, nothing reinvented. The seam is also the FIPS strategy (validated crypto lives behind it). Supply-chain integrity

These pages double as the LLM corpus (§7): the "why" content is exactly what users ask the assistant when a command fails and they want to understand the guard they hit, not just silence it.

7 · The knowledge assistant — surfacing the corpus through Dify

The accrued docs are already a knowledge base; a Dify RAG chat flow turns them into an install-and-operate helper. Full build spec (ingestion, chunking, retrieval, model, system prompt, evaluation) lives in docs/dify-knowledge-assistant.md — the essentials:

DecisionChoiceWhy
CorpusDurable docs/ only — never docs/history/ or docs/analysis/ Journals and dated snapshots would answer with superseded state. operations-faq.md is the highest-value source — already Q→A→prevention shaped.
RetrievalHybrid (vector + keyword) + rerank → top-4, citations on Install answers are full of exact tokens (FEDMGR_ALLOW_ORIGINS, :9443) that pure semantic search fuzzes; a trust tool shows its sources.
ModelClaude Haiku 4.5 at temperature 0; escalate hard architecture Qs to Sonnet 5 Grounded doc-QA is extract-and-synthesize, not open reasoning — Haiku is fast, cheap, and (unlike the Claude 5 family) still accepts temperature, which must be pinned to 0 for faithful answers.
PromptStrict grounding contract: answer only from context, quote commands verbatim, cite source_path, honest "not in the docs" Faithfulness is the failure mode that matters for a trust tool's own helper — an invented flag is worse than "I don't know".
Refresh & evalRe-sync KB on merge to main; eval seeded from the FAQ's Q/A pairs Same discipline as the test suite — green must mean grounded, not just fluent; the corpus is the docs, so stale docs = stale bot.

This is the concrete instance of the "opt-in Dify guided tier" that was Phase 3 on the roadmap below — now specified against the real corpus. It sits alongside the in-browser RVF-WASM Q&A (§5): the WASM path is the zero-infra homepage widget; Dify is the deeper, hosted, install-support surface.
